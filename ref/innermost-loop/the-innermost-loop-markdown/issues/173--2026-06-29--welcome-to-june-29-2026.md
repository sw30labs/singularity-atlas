# Welcome to June 29, 2026

[![Welcome to June 29, 2026](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n2aO!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F24a8d78a-14f7-48f0-9077-ce4732f1a3aa_1983x793.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!n2aO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F24a8d78a-14f7-48f0-9077-ce4732f1a3aa_1983x793.png)

The Singularity now sets the release calendar. Elon Musk framed [Grok v9 as a “solid workhorse in the same league as Opus,”](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2071323738201837785) noting that the flawed v8 foundation (Grok 4.3, a 0.5T model that finished training back in December) makes the coming jump feel enormous, while [Cursor’s data and engineers are quietly folded into v9 post-training](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2071357162195132454) ahead of a 2T run landing in August. The frontier is now leaky enough that sleuths can [fingerprint a grayscale GPT-5.6-sol inside Codex by its “juice” value](https://x.com/lentils80/status/2071343368425406475), and word is [Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 may clear for public release](https://x.com/vraserx/status/2071304121555243061) next week, even abroad, the delay having starved overseas labs of a month of distillation.

That month matters because the gap is thin. [GLM-5.2 is being hailed as “the open-source Claude moment,”](https://x.com/yuchenj_uw/status/2071278256817574297) with demand so fierce that companies rush to post-train and own their own weights. [Ethan Mollick’s new consulting-style benchmark](https://x.com/emollick/status/2071449550662127927) shows the frontier curve climbing fast with only a three-month lag between American and Chinese open models. Former Meta PM [Xiaoyin Qu warns of the true nightmare](https://x.com/quxiaoyin/status/2071489281747800246), Beijing owning the model, chip, and inference layers at once, and argues export controls miss the point: fund open source, lure those models onto NVIDIA, and build nuclear immediately. Meta, fearing that Claude Code and Codex output could seep into its own training data and trigger escalations, is reportedly [capping engineers’ use of both](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/internal-docs-show-meta-putting-limits-claude-codex-fearing-distillation) as it builds MetaCode.

Sovereignty is becoming a product. [Palantir and NVIDIA are shipping an engine to run Nemotron open models](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260629390275/en/Palantir-Launches-Engine-for-Deploying-NVIDIA-Nemotron-Open-Models-in-Sovereign-Environments) in air-gapped, classified environments so agencies can adapt them behind the airlock while keeping data, IP, and weights in-house. [Austria is lobbying the EU to physically host Anthropic](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-28/austria-lobbies-eu-to-host-anthropic-after-us-access-curbs) inside the bloc after a US directive cut foreigners off from its best models, even as [Governor Newsom hands every California agency Claude at a 50% discount](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/29/governor-newsom-announces-a-first-of-its-kind-partnership-providing-anthropic-tools-to-state-agencies-and-improving-services-for-californians/) plus free workforce training. To referee the agents doing all this work, [Senator Mark Warner’s draft bill would impose a “duty of loyalty”](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/sen-mark-warner-unveil-ai-agent-bill) on services like OpenClaw and stop platforms from throttling their rivals.

The catch is that intelligence runs on atoms. [Ming-Chi Kuo says the memory gap will widen through 2027](https://medium.com/@mingchikuo/the-memory-supply-demand-gap-will-keep-widening-through-2027-84ca2c2ed130) as data centers devour consumer supply, the real reason Apple is lobbying to keep China’s CXMT off the Entity List. South Korea is answering with a [$585 billion semiconductor megacomplex](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/politics/20260629/korea-to-invest-585-bil-to-build-semiconductor-complex-in-southwestern-region). [Epoch’s Josh You noted the five biggest labs still used under half the world’s AI compute](https://epochai.substack.com/p/frontier-labs-dont-use-most-ai-compute) at the end of 2025, yet Anthropic and OpenAI could swallow that headroom within a few years. [Firmus and DayOne are pouring a 360-MW Nvidia campus into Indonesia](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-28/ai-startup-firmus-to-build-indonesia-data-center-with-nvidia) under a deal worth up to $30 billion in offtake, while Polaroid [trolls the whole boom with a billboard](https://www.businessinsider.com/polaroid-anti-ai-data-centers-water-ad-2026-6) urging you to jump in the water before the data centers drink it.

Humans are being re-org’d around the machines. Claude Code creator [Boris Cherny sees engineering, product, and design melting into five archetypes](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2071379474277613732), the prototyper, builder, sweeper, grower, and maintainer, none tied to a job title. [A Deloitte town hall reportedly projects human consultant billing shrinking to a sliver by 2035](https://www.wsj.com/cfo-journal/inside-consultants-messy-shift-from-hourly-billing-7bd9b802) as agents take the majority, and [Erik Brynjolfsson’s new Canaries Dashboard](https://fortune.com/2026/06/27/what-is-ai-impact-entry-level-jobs-stanford-adp-canaries-brynjolfsson-richardson/), tracking 4.6 million workers, extends his earlier canaries-in-the-coal-mine finding that AI’s roughly 13% hit to entry-level employment for 22-to-25-year-olds “isn’t going away.”

Culture is the next thing to be synthesized. [Animation is feeling AI’s blow first](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-06-28/animation-s-ai-reckoning-filmmakers-say-they-can-make-movies-for-90-less), with one studio planning a feature using 40 animators for about $15 million instead of $100-200 million, even as a filmmaker quit an Amazon AI project in protest. [Instagram now aims ads built from your profile picture at your friends](https://x.com/venturetwins/status/2071277885646868536), and [GPT-5.6 Pro one-shotted a legally-distinct Pokemon clone](https://x.com/chetaslua/status/2068733063329456499) in 31 minutes from a short prompt.

Which makes authenticity the next scarce resource, and detection the business supplying it. East Asian students are [reading exam answers off AI smart glasses](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/26/asia/ai-glasses-cheating-exams-intl-hnk), a [Brown professor caught at least 50 students](https://english.elpais.com/education/2026-06-28/ai-fraud-at-brown-university-academic-integrity-is-at-risk.html) whose take-home average of 96 cratered to 48 once the final went in-person, and a blogger let [Claude Opus 4.8 overrule his doctor’s torn-tendon diagnosis](https://antoine.fi/mri-analysis-using-claude-code-opus), then realized he had no idea whom to believe. Tidal answered in kind, [tagging fully AI-generated songs](https://tidal.com/ai-policy) from mid-July and cutting royalties to anything it deems wholly machine-made.

All of which quietly reprices what is worth owning. [Rocket Lab is buying Iridium for about $8 billion](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rocket-lab-to-acquire-iridium-in-historic-deal-creating-a-fully-vertically-integrated-space-powerhouse-primed-for-growth-302813075.html) to own the entire space stack, even as the [Magnificent 7 underperform and their P/E premium sinks to a decade low](https://www.apollo.com/content/dam/apolloaem/pdf/daily-spark/2026/jun/28/062826-Mag7.pdf). The same inversion is reaching the front door: with [renting now cheaper than owning in every big metro](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/28/some-renters-say-homeownership-isnt-part-of-their-american-dream.html), even millionaires rent and ask whether owning anything matters, in an age when the asset everyone races to own is a set of model weights. The one thing no one can secure is protein, as [GLP-1 demand has halved whey inventories](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/28/america-cant-get-enough-protein-the-dairy-industry-cant-keep-up.html) and sold suppliers out for the year.

Where there’s a will, there’s a whey.
