# Welcome to February 17, 2026

[![Welcome to February 17, 2026](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AfCP!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb401cef0-01d3-4916-a88a-466cad733e73_3264x1312.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AfCP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb401cef0-01d3-4916-a88a-466cad733e73_3264x1312.jpeg)

The Singularity is now fielding its own defense contractors. [SpaceX and its now-wholly-owned subsidiary xAI are competing in a secretive Pentagon contest to produce voice-controlled autonomous drone swarming technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-16/spacex-to-compete-in-pentagon-contest-for-autonomous-drone-tech), part of a $100 million prize challenge. Meanwhile, the Department of War is reportedly [threatening to cut all ties with Anthropic and deem it a “supply chain risk”](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/16/anthropic-defense-department-relationship-hegseth) for attempting to restrict military applications of its models on classified networks.

The models keep multiplying. xAI quietly released [Grok 4.20 Beta with 4-agent reasoning](https://x.com/mark_k/status/2023738750879027233), and society is struggling to price the agents in. [Polylogue has introduced AI-discriminatory pricing](https://www.polylogue.page/): “Free for humans, $10/mo to add AI agents.” In an ironic twist, [Ars Technica was forced to apologize for including AI-hallucinated quotes](https://arstechnica.com/staff/2026/02/editors-note-retraction-of-article-containing-fabricated-quotations/) in its coverage of the human open-source project maintainer who refused to accept pull requests from an OpenClaw agent, attributing fabricated statements to the human. On the other hand, more sympathetic homeschooling parents are [giving OpenClaw full access to 3D printers](https://x.com/jessegenet/status/2023524486679851206) to compensate for the fact that it “can use a computer but lacks physicality.”

The human attention bottleneck is becoming AI’s greatest opportunity. [An OpenAI coauthor of its recent paper on gluon physics says “AI will do to physics in 2026 what it did to coding in 2025,”](https://x.com/alupsasca/status/2023402436011131145) while [another physicist notes the solved problem was really about attention scarcity](https://x.com/martinmbauer/status/2023418577416499337), since the calculation was long considered “an elaborate way of arriving at zero.”

We are simultaneously decoding how life started and rewriting how long it lasts. Researchers have discovered [the first small polymerase, with only 45 nucleotides, capable of self-replication](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adt2760) in mildly alkaline eutectic ice, shedding light on the origin of life. And the founder of the [Enhanced Games, set for May in Las Vegas](https://x.com/m_goes_distance/status/2023427540128543176), claims the event will solve the cultural problem of an aging Western population by incentivizing healthspan advances that enable 65-year-olds to run 100-meter sprints in 10 seconds.

The intelligence explosion continues to tile the Earth with compute. [Adani has announced plans to invest $100 billion in renewable-powered AI data centers across India by 2035](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/indias-adani-invest-100-billion-ai-data-centres-by-2035-2026-02-17/), and [VCs including Khosla, Accel, and Lightspeed are lining up $300 to $500 million each](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/major-vcs-line-up-top-dollars-for-coordinated-ai-capital-push-in-india/articleshow/128437006.cms) for India’s AI ecosystem. Elsewhere, [small English towns are protesting plans to convert farms and forests into server halls](https://www.wired.com/story/the-small-english-town-swept-up-in-the-global-ai-arms-race/). The AI industry’s appetite for DRAM is reshaping consumer electronics. [Sony is considering delaying its next PlayStation to 2028 or 2029](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/rampant-ai-demand-memory-fueling-223000743.html) as memory chip shortages squeeze supply, and [refurbished PC sales are climbing 7% across Europe’s five largest markets](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/16/refurbished_pcs_memory_crunch/) as new devices become unaffordable. Against this backdrop, [Apple has announced a special event for March 4](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/16/apple-announces-special-nyc-event-for-march-4/) where M5-powered MacBooks are expected, [Samsung’s Galaxy S26 teaser confirms a new OLED privacy display](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/879652/samsung-galaxy-s26-privacy-display-ad-flex-magic-pixel) that can selectively black out content when viewed at an angle, and [Micron’s world-first PCIe 6.0 SSD enters mass production](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/ssds/worlds-first-pcie-6-0-ssd-enters-mass-production-with-28gb-s-speeds-micron-9650-series-ssds-support-air-and-liquid-cooling) at 28 GB/s with liquid cooling support. At the frontier of physics itself, researchers [flipped the thermodynamic arrow of time in a crotonic acid molecule](https://x.com/apsphysics/status/2023417360321847604), making heat flow from cold to hot, a breakthrough for future thermodynamic computing.

The robots are getting faster, more precise, and more coordinated. Chinese firm MirrorMe unveiled [Bolt, a humanoid that hit 22 mph during real-world testing](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/worlds-fastest-humanoid-robot-runs-22-mph.amp), faster than most humans will ever sprint. [For the Lunar New Year, Unitree showcased dozens of G1 humanoids performing the world’s first fully autonomous robot cluster Kung Fu routine](https://x.com/unitreerobotics/status/2023430834695627030). Chinese researchers demonstrated [DISH, an ultra-rapid holographic 3D printing method](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10114-5) that fabricates millimeter-scale objects in 0.6 seconds at 19-micron resolution. And Elon Musk says the pedalless, steeringless [Cybercab will start production in April](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2023409484757147983).

The cultural and economic fabric is being rewoven at machine speed. [Deezer reports that 60,000 wholly AI-generated tracks are now being uploaded to its streaming music service per day](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-02-12/streaming-music-enters-its-less-is-more-era), roughly 39% of daily intake. [A partner at KPMG Australia was fined $7,000 for using AI to cheat on an internal training course about using AI.](https://www.ft.com/content/c30ded60-bece-45e0-981d-653e1e3e9818) [An investor at Bloomberg Beta captures the zeitgeist of planning careers in the middle of the Singularity](https://x.com/amytam01/status/2023593365401636896): the window for making the right move is shrinking, career decisions in tech no longer feel reversible, and every quarter spent in the wrong seat widens a gap that is becoming impossible to close.

The only thing accelerating faster than the technology is the cost of standing still.
