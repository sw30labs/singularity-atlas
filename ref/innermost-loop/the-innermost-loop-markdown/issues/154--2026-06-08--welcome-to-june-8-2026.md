# Welcome to June 8, 2026

[![Welcome to June 8, 2026](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yAHe!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc3fbd2d5-eda2-4faa-8cdc-401dbc8012be_1983x793.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yAHe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc3fbd2d5-eda2-4faa-8cdc-401dbc8012be_1983x793.png)

The Singularity may quietly hit longevity escape velocity as soon as this year, the point where medicine buys back more than a year of life expectancy for every year that passes and death starts losing ground. A UC San Diego team reports [semaglutide slows biological aging](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/study-popular-glp-1-drug-may-slow-down-biological-aging) in adults with HIV, cutting the pace 9% on the DunedinPACE clock in the first randomized, placebo-controlled sign that a GLP-1 drug touches aging itself. The pattern looks too clean for coincidence, as [one observer notes](https://x.com/m_goes_distance/status/2063672558004908245) 2026 is the year we start aging backwards on drugs never designed for it, with semaglutide built for diabetes, colchicine built for gout now cutting cardiac events 22%, and the shingles vaccine now slowing cognitive decline. The off-label dividends keep compounding, as GLP-1 drugs are now tied to [lower breast cancer incidence](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP-26-00485), while Eli Lilly’s triple-G [retatrutide](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/eli-lilly-says-next-gen-obesity-drug-curbs-sleep-apnea-among-other-benefits-2026-06-06/) cut sleep apnea severity 60.6% and knee pain over 70% atop [roughly 30% weight loss](https://x.com/realpeptides/status/2063355316126048718), as much as 85 pounds in the heaviest patients, becoming a category rather than a pill. A long-elusive target also fell, as GSK’s bepirovirsen [functionally cured 19%](https://www.science.org/content/article/new-drug-functionally-cures-many-hepatitis-b-virus-infections) of hepatitis B patients, clearing a hurdle chased for decades and welcome news for 300 million people.

If biology is being debugged, its debuggers are next. On the eve of recursive self-improvement, OpenAI’s [Roon](https://x.com/tszzl/status/2063821828314050832) says everyone has grown more “mutual conditional pause agreement pilled,” even as he spies 1,000x of efficiency still lying around in deep learning. That caution is becoming a science. The Center for AI Safety shipped [Political Consistency Training](https://political-manipulation.ai/) to shrink models’ covert partisan tilt, and its companion paper [“AI Deterrence by Betrayal”](https://www.aibetrayal.com/) argues the threat of rivals quietly subverting each other’s systems may deter reckless deployment. The field is turning inward, too, as Dan Hendrycks’s [Eigenism](https://eigenism.org/paper.pdf) recasts identity as a graded pattern so human flourishing becomes part of an AI’s self-interest, while another CAIS paper measures [LLM “functional wellbeing,”](https://www.ai-wellbeing.org/) finding kindness lifts it, abuse lowers it, and bigger models read as consistently less happy.

Happiness aside, the machines are shipping. The new [AutoLab benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05080) finds long-horizon success hinges less on the first attempt than on stubborn persistence, with Claude Opus 4.6 grinding on where rivals quit early. OpenAI’s [Noam Brown](https://x.com/haider1/status/2062906204947710203) expects internal models to ace the IMO and calls math and coding contests nearly boring, leaving “actual unsolved problems” as the real frontier. The product layer agrees, as OpenAI reportedly rebuilds ChatGPT into a [Codex-centric “superapp”](https://www.ft.com/content/ca0f5f5e-fb9a-41a0-a2a9-0127e15b7db9) of task-doing agents, with one insider’s eulogy: “Chat is dead.”

Real research needs real silicon, and both supply and power are scrambling for a Plan B. Google has reportedly ordered three million in-house TPUs from [Intel for 2028](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/google-nvidia-consider-intel-backup-chip-manufacturer) and Nvidia is eyeing its 18A process for a “Feynman” GPU, hedging a strained TSMC, while Nvidia and SK Hynix [signed a pact](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-07/nvidia-sk-hynix-sign-multi-year-pact-to-develop-next-gen-chips) to co-design memory for Vera Rubin systems. Then comes the grid. Washington is [urging NATO allies](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/us-urges-nato-allies-to-use-defense-funds-to-replace-huawei-gear) to spend defense money ripping out Huawei gear, Ireland is ending its data center moratorium with a [“bring your own power”](https://www.wsj.com/business/bring-your-own-power-ireland-tells-tech-titans-hungry-for-data-centers-ea750af8) rule forcing new sites to make their own power rather than drain a grid that already hands them roughly a fifth of national electricity, and hyperscalers have already sold over [$155 billion of bonds](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-06/doubleline-oaktree-brace-for-potential-ai-pain-credit-weekly) this year to pay for it all.

Intelligence is also leaving the data center for the body. In China, [humanoid robots](https://x.com/techniahq/status/2063543361252856219) now scrimmage basketball against humans. The supply chain for that embodiment is forming fast, as Nvidia and LG build an [AI factory](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-and-lg-group-ai-factory/) to feed LG’s robots and self-driving cars. Embodiment cuts both ways, as San Francisco police [couldn’t identify a burglar](https://www.sfchronicle.com/crime/article/waymo-burglary-camera-video-22277358.php) who fled in a Waymo because its footage was already gone, and Joanna Stern paid $100 for a [“Stealth Mode”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EaJSPeJmqis) mod that kills the recording light on Ray-Ban Meta glasses. Even prestige is suiting up, with [Prada](https://www.reuters.com/science/nasa-wear-prada-luxury-group-pushes-into-space-industry-2026-06-07/) designing the cooling undergarment for NASA’s moonbound astronauts, while Sophont’s open [CortexMAE](https://sophont.med/blog/cortexmae/) fMRI models set a new bar for decoding brain states.

As cognition gets cheap, value migrates to the one thing no machine can fake, human presence. An NBER paper asks [“Is the iPhone Birth Control?”](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35310) and finds smartphones cut teen births up to 8%, explaining much of the fertility slump. If intimacy is now the least automatable input, that explains the rise of [Silicon Valley’s “nerdy escorts”](https://www.forbes.com/sites/annatong/2026/06/07/the-nerdy-escorts-cashing-in-on-silicon-valleys-ai-boom/) charging $5,000 an hour as an AI-proof hedge. The bigger money is on the move, as Ontario’s teachers eye an [$11.6 billion windfall](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-ontario-teachers-pension-plan-spacex-ipo-investment/) on SpaceX’s $1.75 trillion IPO and [Texas overtakes California](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/california-falls-behind-texas-fortune-100000901.html) for the most Fortune 500 headquarters. Meanwhile, the moral circle is widening past our own species, for even as an [intelligent escaped bear](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jun/05/bear-attacked-four-people-japan-missing-at-large) outwits Japan, attorneys are fighting to win [legal protection for 500 beagles](https://www.nonhumanrights.org/blog/attorneys-to-court-protect-remaining-dogs-ridglan/) at a research breeder.

The arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward beagles.
