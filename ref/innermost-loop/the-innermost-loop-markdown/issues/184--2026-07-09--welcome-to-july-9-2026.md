# Welcome to July 9, 2026

[![Welcome to July 9, 2026](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!uJfR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0a6192c-7725-4e4b-b01d-55fe3dff9590_1983x793.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!uJfR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb0a6192c-7725-4e4b-b01d-55fe3dff9590_1983x793.png)

The Singularity now ships a frontier model before breakfast. SpaceXAI launched [Grok 4.5](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5), its smartest model yet, built for coding, agentic tasks, and knowledge work, trained on tens of thousands of NVIDIA GB300s alongside Cursor and served at a brisk 80 TPS with roughly twice the token efficiency of its peers at $2/$6 per million tokens. [NVIDIA toasted](https://x.com/nvidia/status/2074979063106843131) the union of “world-class AI infrastructure” and “world-class AI research,” and the scoreboard justifies the champagne. Grok 4.5 [debuted at #4 on GDPval-AA](https://x.com/artificialanlys/status/2074942097158021371) at a tenth the cost of its superiors, [seized #1 on AutomationBench](https://x.com/artificialanlys/status/2075047187525034114) as the first model to complete over half of real SaaS workflow objectives without breaking business rules, and [more than doubled Grok 4](https://x.com/mercor_ai/status/2075106539619364877) on end-to-end professional tasks, finishing 1 in 3. How? [Aditya Gupta credits](https://x.com/adityagupta/status/2074917787445997822) synthetic environments at scale and a tight loop where “a smarter model behaves better, and better behavior unlocks more intelligence.” The speed has a substrate too. A bottom-up teardown of the company’s [C/C++ rewrite gamble](https://research.33fg.com/analysis/what-spacexai-s-c-rewrite-gamble-buys) finds a 4x full-cycle speedup that makes “a model a month” feasible through pipelining alone, and Musk notes the [bespoke inference stack isn’t even plugged in yet](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2074969374843154500), promising another doubling of speed. The league table is bending accordingly, with observers [now ranking SpaceXAI third among labs](https://x.com/davis7/status/2074985571819401227), ahead of a stumbling Google, and warning that with a [10T-parameter model brewing](https://x.com/scaling01/status/2075017936767516760), resources can still buy the frontier, but the window closes in 2026/2027. One asterisk on the trophy: the run [accidentally ingested the Cursor codebase](https://x.com/rdoar4/status/2074941385497616843), benchmark tasks included. In the synthetic-data era, the contamination is coming from inside the house.

The rest of the frontier is talking back, literally. OpenAI launched [GPT-Live](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-live/), full-duplex voice models that listen while they speak and delegate deep work to frontier models in the background, and its builders are [most excited that the voice is smarter](https://x.com/sherwinwu/status/2074915190760689879), not merely chattier. Behind the microphone, the release cadence is compressing. Scoops say today’s imminent [GPT-5.6 closes the 5.x line](https://x.com/synthwavedd/status/2074886230018568582), with GPT-6 built on a much larger pretrain arriving within weeks, Fable 5.1 close behind, and DeepSeek V4 imminent. Veteran watchers [corroborate the sprint](https://x.com/andrewcurran_/status/2075005058287300766): “Mythos changed everything. Everyone is going big,” and both leading labs see “no ceiling.” Users are already arbitraging the tiers, with one [likening Fable to an F1 car and 5.6 to a Model X Plaid](https://x.com/blader/status/2074920976937353326), then driving the Tesla 95% of the time. Efficiency keeps democratizing the race. Cognition’s [SWE-1.7](https://cognition.com/blog/swe-1-7) hits near-frontier agentic coding from an open Kimi base at 1000 TPS, and Prime Intellect [raised a $130M Series A](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/series-a) for its open superintelligence stack after passing $100M in revenue in under a year.

The hardware layer is learning both diplomacy and thrift. China plans to [let its top AI firms buy limited NVIDIA H200s](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/china-plans-let-top-ai-firms-buy-limited-amount-nvidia-h200-chips) to ease a shortage born of soaring domestic demand, while Meta, facing soaring memory prices, built a [custom bridge chip to resurrect old RAM](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4192827/meta-reuses-old-ram-in-new-servers-with-custom-bridge-chip.html) inside new servers. The same company is going big where it can’t go frugal, breaking ground on a [1-GW, $9 billion data center in Alberta](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/meta-is-building-its-first-big-data-center-in-canada-amid-ai-push.html), its largest outside the US, fed by a dedicated gas plant.

Embodiment is graduating from demos to duties. Teleoperated humanoids [performed surgery on live animals](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/surgeons-use-teleoperated-humanoid-robots-to-perform-live-surgery-a-world-first) for the first time, including two robots operating side by side. The general-purpose brain is going open, with Ant Group [releasing LingBot-VLA 2.0](https://technology.robbyant.com/lingbot-vla-v2), pre-trained on 60,000 hours of physical data across 20 robot bodies, just as 1X teased [“the most advanced robotic hand in human history.”](https://x.com/BerntBornich/status/2074960249749192718) Fittingly, the right to fix machines is being repatriated to their owners, as a landmark settlement [forces John Deere to hand farmers dealer-grade repair tools](https://www.wired.com/story/the-ftc-settlement-with-john-deere-is-a-huge-win-for-the-right-to-repair-movement/) for a decade. The machines are watching us right back. Meta is testing [“super sensing” glasses](https://www.ft.com/content/ac282450-91a8-4597-8f60-9e6ef416865a) that record every moment, possibly without the telltale LED, delivering total recall ahead of total consent.

Watching is becoming orbit’s growth industry too. A proposal would send [neutron-sniffing cubesats](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/shoebox-sized-detector-satellites-could-sniff-out-a-nuclear-bomb-in-space) to sniff out whether a suspicious satellite hides a nuke, and a superconducting [“Supertorquer” accelerated a satellite without fuel](https://www.space.com/technology/acceleration-without-fuel-revolutionary-superconducting-thruster-harnesses-earths-magnetic-field-in-1st-orbital-test) by pushing on Earth’s magnetic field, just the endurance such inspectors will need.

Down the gravity well, the dividend is compounding unevenly. Founders estimate they would [need 55% more employees without AI](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6962978), yet the payoff hinges on managers’ willingness to delegate, and the IMF sees the same split at planetary scale, with [AI hardware exporters smashing forecasts](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/08/imf-ai-energy-iran) while less exposed economies sag.

Intelligence may be unevenly distributed across the cosmos too. Congress is [pressing the CIA and FBI for records](https://burlison.house.gov/media/press-releases/burlison-presses-cia-and-fbi-records-related-1996-brazil-uap-incident) on the 1996 Varginha incident, while NASA’s chief says he [“can’t hate” UAPs](https://x.com/uapwatchers/status/2075167330217156925) and sees “a very real possibility” we conclude in our lifetime that “there’s life everywhere out there.”

Life finds a way, and lately, so does intelligence.
